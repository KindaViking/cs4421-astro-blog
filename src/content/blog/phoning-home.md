---
title: "How Rovers Phone Home"
description: "Deep Space Network antennas, orbiter relays, and a long speed-of-light delay."
pubDate: 2026-09-29
author: callum-b
---

A rover cannot simply call Earth. Signals take roughly 5 to 20 minutes one way,
depending on where the planets are, so rovers drive themselves between
commands using onboard autonomy.

Most data is sent to orbiters such as MRO, which relay it onward at much higher
speeds than a direct link. On Earth, NASA's Deep Space Network uses antennas
in California, Spain, and Australia, spaced so that one can always see the
spacecraft as the planet rotates.
